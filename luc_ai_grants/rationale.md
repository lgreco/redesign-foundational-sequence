# Rationale: Why This Project Fits the CFP

Status: draft, in progress — feeds the Project Description and internal framing, not a submission document itself.

## Category: Programmatic, not Impact Research

I'm applying under Programmatic Curricular Innovations ($20,000), not Impact Research ($5,000), even though we're already practicing human-centered AI approaches and could make a case for the smaller category. COMP 158/159 hits the Programmatic target three ways at once: it's a first-year course sequence, it's an intro requirement feeding multiple programs, and it replaces more than one course. Impact Research would undersell what is, in effect, a curricular replacement already in motion. Feasibility is one of four scored criteria, and most applicants will be proposing something they haven't started — we can point to a completed pilot.

## The objection I have to answer, not avoid

The hardest question a mission-grounded review board will raise is about our agent-based work — agents conducting oral exams, agents grading student work. Read uncharitably, that's outsourcing the evaluative relationship between teacher and student, which is precisely what a human-centric AI framework should guard against. I'm not going to pretend that objection isn't real.

The answer is available and it's strong: an agent-conducted oral exam is *more* human contact than the autograder it replaces, not less. In a large-enrollment CS1, the realistic alternative to agent-mediated orals isn't a professor sitting with each student — it's a unit test suite scoring a submission nobody has to defend aloud. Agent-conducted orals restore *viva voce* assessment at a scale where it had otherwise become impossible. The faculty member retains judgment over what the exam measures and how it's scored; the student is still obliged to explain their own reasoning, out loud, to something that can push back. That maps directly onto the reflection and evaluation moments of the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm, which the CFP explicitly invites us to name. I want to frame this as recovering something human that scale had eliminated — that's not a defense of the practice, it's the thesis of the proposal.

## Using the encyclical precisely, not transactionally

I don't want to gesture at *Magnifica Humanitas* the way a lot of applicants will gesture at "the mission." Leo XIV's premise is specific: technology is neither antagonistic to humanity nor inherently evil, but it is never neutral, because "it takes on the characteristics of those who devise, finance, regulate, and use it." For an intro programming sequence, that line is a gift — it's the philosophical warrant for teaching first-year CS students that the artifacts they build carry the values of their makers. That's a curricular claim I can build assignments and reflection prompts around, not just a devotional flourish at the top of the proposal. Specificity like that is what will separate this application from ones that name-check the mission without doing anything with it.

## Where AI should be disallowed

The CFP explicitly invites "careful consideration of when to disallow the use of AI in a curriculum," and I take that as seriously as the adoption side. Not every use of AI in COMP 158/159 should default to permitted — some assignments and activities exist specifically to build skills (debugging by hand, reading a stack trace, reasoning through recursion on paper) that AI assistance would let students skip past without acquiring. Deciding where to prohibit AI is as deliberate a design act as deciding where to use it, and the proposal should name both sides of that decision, not just the adoption half.

## Timing and dependencies

Two things are time-sensitive and worth deciding now rather than closer to the December opening. First, the CFD's Human-Centered AI Digital Badge runs in Fall 2026 — if I want that on the application as evidence of preparation, enrolling is a decision for this month, not later. Second, the letter of support: replacing existing intro courses is exactly the "curricular changes affect complex programmatic structures" case the CFD flags, so I need my chair aligned well before the December opening, not scrambling for a signature in January.
